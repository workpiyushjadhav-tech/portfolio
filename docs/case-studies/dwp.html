DWP Bereavement Redesign

A service model that proactively reaches bereaved people with the right financial entitlements at the right time, without making them navigate four government departments in acute grief.

Challenge

The Challenge

This is not a digital problem. It is a systemic coordination failure.

The Bereavement Support Payment (BSP) must be claimed within 3 months for the full amount, and claims up to 21 months after the death get fewer payments. I found no proactive government communication about it, so discovery falls to the individual when their capacity is lowest.

When someone in the UK loses a partner or family member, they become eligible for entitlements across DWP, HMRC, local councils and the NHS, yet no single authoritative service tells them what they qualify for. At least four departments each have their own eligibility criteria, application routes and deadlines. Tell Us Once exists at death registration but is underused, and registrars, GPs and funeral directors, who are high-trust contacts, lack the tools to signpost accurately.

Secondary research surfaced nine findings that shaped the problem statement. One: people find out what support exists only once they need it, and high satisfaction scores in existing data reflect only the people who succeeded. Those who never reached the service are invisible.

Users switch between websites, helplines and offline services with little continuity across channels. Vulnerable groups (digitally excluded, low-literacy, disabled) face the greatest barriers at the worst possible moment.

Secondary research synthesis, UK bereavement service landscape

The brief: Redesign the service so bereaved people get the right information at the right time, without navigating a fragmented system in acute grief. The service should initiate contact rather than wait to be found.

Approach

My Approach

An ethical pivot. Recruiting recently bereaved participants within five weeks raised significant ethical concerns. Rather than run methodologically compromised interviews, I proposed and led a large-scale content analysis of public Reddit bereavement posts.

Method: Braun & Clarke’s (2006) six-phase thematic analysis. Communities: r/UKPersonalFinance, r/LegalAdviceUK, r/bereavement. Data handling: public posts only, with usernames removed, and ethics documentation in place.

The standout finding. The dominant keyword across all three communities was “advice”, not “information”, “forms” or “benefits”. My reading: people were turning to peers because official sources weren’t giving direction. The system had information but lacked guidance.

Frequency is not always a proxy for significance. In my reading, the smallest theme, Lack of Proactive Information, was the most strategically significant: people in these posts seemed to have stopped expecting the system to reach out.

Research moves

Three research moves

Three research moves that shaped the brief.

01

Ethical pivot: large-scale content analysis

  • More data than interviews could have produced in five weeks

What it cost: people who post online aren’t a representative sample of bereaved people, so I treat the findings as directional.

02

Contextual mapping: the clarifying artefact

I traced every bereavement touchpoint before a user could reach BSP. There was no single “start here” point, and no formal touchpoint that mentions the 3-month window.

  • The map put the fragmentation on one surface, with eligibility rules scattered across at least four agencies
  • It directly generated the three-layer structure
03

Competitor analysis and analogous inspiration

I reviewed bereavement service models across four countries and analogous services from other sectors. One pattern held across the high performers: the service initiates contact rather than waiting to be found.

  • Macmillan Cancer Support: staging complexity into clear phases
  • NHS Symptom Checker: question-based, personalised next steps
  • e-Estonia: enter once, no repeated context

Solution

The Solution

Bereaved people aren’t one user group, so no single solution serves everyone. The model’s strength is complementarity: each layer covers a moment the others miss.

Key decision: one product or three layers

  • Single GOV.UK page redesignInsufficient. Helps confident users and misses those who never reach GOV.UK. Survivorship bias built in.
  • Phone helpline improvementInsufficient. High-trust but reactive. People still have to know to call, and it doesn’t fix the zero-touchpoint problem.
  • Intermediary-only toolInsufficient. A force multiplier, but registrars see only one moment, so people between touchpoints still have no support.
  • SelectedThree-layer service modelA proactive letter, a digital triage tool and an intermediary dashboard. Complementary layers serve different users at different moments.

Cost: More moving parts, and none of the three layers has been tested with users.

Layers

Three layers, each covering a different moment

Proactive notification letter

Triggered at death registration through Tell Us Once, so the system reaches out and you don’t have to find it. An automated, personalised letter (and SMS where a number is held) arrives within 5 to 10 working days of registration, listing 3 to 5 entitlements by name with pre-calculated deadlines and a single contact route for each.

  • Designed to read as official, personalised and time-sensitive rather than junk mail (not tested)
  • Delivery options include BSL, translated and large print formats
  • Built on the existing Tell Us Once trigger rather than new infrastructure (I haven’t verified it can trigger a letter)

Five-question digital triage tool

Accessible from GOV.UK, no account required. Five questions cover relationship to the deceased, NI contribution status, dependent children, current benefit status and date of death (to auto-calculate deadlines). The output is a ranked, personalised checklist with a deadline countdown and one-click paths to start each claim.

  • Progressive disclosure: no information shown before it’s needed
  • No dead ends: every path leads to an action or a human contact
  • Channel agnostic: printable, saveable, usable via phone relay

Intermediary-first dashboard

For registrars, GPs, funeral directors and Citizens Advice workers. One trained registrar serves everyone who registers a death in their office. A lightweight dashboard takes client type and date of death and returns an entitlement matrix with deadline indicators, flagging when the BSP window is within 30 days.

  • Bereaved people already trust these professionals at the point of contact
  • Covers all four departments in one view, not per agency
  • A layer on existing professional touchpoints, not new infrastructure

Swipe the panel, or use the tabs

The design principles were consistent across all three layers: the service initiates contact rather than waiting for users to find it. No dead ends. No navigation without direction. No deadline without a clear path to act on it.

Impact

Measurable Impact

There’s no measured impact yet. The solution has never been put in front of a bereaved person, so this section sets out what the evidence supports and what it doesn’t.

What the evidence supports

  • The problem statement: Grounded in 13,000+ coded sentences from public Reddit posts (eight themes, 41 codes, Braun & Clarke six-phase method). Reddit posters aren’t a representative sample.
  • Precedent for the direction: Competitor analysis and analogous services from other sectors point to services that initiate contact. That’s precedent, not proof it works here.
  • Tell Us Once exists and is underused: As far as I checked. Whether it can trigger the Layer 1 letter is untested.

What it doesn’t support yet

  • Live usability testing: Not conducted. The two unknowns below remain open, and testing is required before any deployment.

Unknowns

What only testing can answer

Two things only real testing can answer.

Unknown 01

Does the Layer 1 letter actually get read?

Risk
Older bereaved spouses, the most likely recipients, are the least likely to recognise an unfamiliar DWP letter as relevant. Open rate is unknown.
Next step
Service testing with participants aged 60+, with low digital literacy, and with people who weren’t the primary account holder in their household.
Unknown 02

Do five questions cover eligibility edge cases?

Risk
Cohabiting but unmarried partners, overseas deaths and complex legacy benefit situations weren’t fully mapped through the question flow.
Next step
A dedicated sprint testing the triage tool against non-standard relationship statuses and mixed legacy benefit profiles.

Takeaways

Key takeaways: decisions and what they cost

  • Pivot rather than compromise.

    I chose public-post analysis over rushed interviews with recently bereaved people. It gave more data than five weeks of recruiting could, and the cost is that it isn’t a representative sample.

  • Three layers over one product.

    The contextual map showed no single layer reaches everyone. The cost is more moving parts, and none has been tested.

  • Read for significance, not just frequency.

    The smallest theme (41 sentences) shaped the whole direction. That took interpretation beyond a count, and it’s my reading, not a measured finding.

Reflection

What worked, what didn’t

What worked

  • The ethical pivot. I proposed and led it, and it’s the decision I’m most proud of.
  • A solution that came from the map, not an assumption. Before the mapping exercise I’d have proposed a single GOV.UK page redesign. The map showed no formal touchpoint for the 3-month window, and that no one layer could serve everyone.
  • Building on existing infrastructure. Designing around Tell Us Once as the trigger was meant to keep the solution inside real government constraints, though I haven’t verified it can do the job.

What didn’t, and what’s unresolved

  • No live testing. The prototype was never put in front of a real bereaved person, and research extrapolation can’t close that gap.
  • Frequency isn’t significance. I almost missed the Lack of Proactive Information theme because it was the smallest.
  • Eligibility edge cases. The triage tool works for the standard BSP profile. The edge cases above (cohabiting but unmarried partners, overseas deaths) remain a known gap for a future sprint.
